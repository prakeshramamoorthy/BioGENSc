Risk Assessment

Why Bioremediation Matters: The Bad Impacts of Oil Pollution

Traditional physical cleanups are costly. Here are the devastating ecological and economic threats.

A) Ecological Damage to Malaysia's Coast

1. Damage to Marine Ecosystems:

Oil forms a layer blocking sunlight, shutting down photosynthesis in phytoplankton, corals, and seagrass beds.

2. Harm to Marine Wildlife:

Direct toxic ingestion, abnormal larval development in crabs, and gill blockages in valuable commercial fishes.

3. Biodiversity Loss & Mangroves:

Oil trapped in mangrove pneumatophores suffocates the trees. Full ecological recovery can take over 15 years.

B) Socio-Economic Destruction

1. Fisheries and Aquaculture Losses:

Destroys fish stocks in Terengganu, Johor, and Sabah. Fishing bans severely cripple the income of B40 fishermen.

2. Tourism Decline:

Blackened coastlines force closure of resorts on Tioman, Perhentian, and Sipadan Island marine parks.

3. Escalating Cleanup Costs & Port Delays:

Chemical dispersants cost millions. Blocked shipping lanes disrupt Malaccan and Klang port operations.

Subscale 1

Microbial Process: Decomposers in Ecosystem

Understanding aerobic vs anaerobic bioremediation and chemical equations.

1. Aerobic Bioremediation (with Oxygen)

Hydrocarbon decomposers oxidize carbon targets rapidly in oxygenated setups:

2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O + Energy

Process: Bacteria (Pseudomonas) oxidize hydrocarbons, converting pollutants into Carbon dioxide + Water.

2. Anaerobic Bioremediation (without Oxygen)

Takes place in oxygen-deprived sediments. Microbes utilize nitrate instead of oxygen:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 4NO₃⁻ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + 2N₂ + Energy

Process: Nitrates act as electron acceptors, converting toxic compounds into harmless nitrogen gases.
